What is a Percentage?

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The word "percent" originates from the Latin phrase per centum, which literally translates to "per hundred" or "by the hundred." Percentages are denoted using the percent sign (%) and provide a standardized benchmark for comparing quantities across different scales.

Percentages are universally employed in mathematics, shopping discounts, exam scoring, statistical evaluation, banking interest rates, business performance tracking, and daily financial calculations.

Mathematical Equivalent Example:

25% = 25 / 100 = 0.25 (Decimal Form)

Types of Percentage Calculations You Can Do

Explore the different mathematical operations supported by Calculator Pro with formulas and practical examples.

What is X% of Y?

Determines the specific portion or value represented by a designated percentage of a total amount.

Formula: (X ÷ 100) × Y

Example: What is 20% of 500? (20 ÷ 100) × 500 = 100.

Percentage Increase Calculator

Measures the percentage relative growth from an initial starting value to a final higher value.

Formula: ((New Value - Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100

Example: Price rises from $100 to $125. Growth = 25%.

Percentage Decrease Calculator

Calculates the percentage reduction or loss from an original quantity down to a lower figure.

Formula: ((Old Value - New Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100

Example: Value drops from $200 to $150. Reduction = 25%.

Percentage Difference / Change

Compares two values relative to their average when neither value is explicitly the baseline.

Formula: (|A - B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2)) × 100

Example: Difference between 10 and 15 is 40%.

Percentage of a Number

Finds what percentage a specific part represents in comparison to a whole total value.

Formula: (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Example: 25 out of 200 represents 12.5%.

Reverse Percentage & Error

Back-calculates original pre-tax or pre-discount totals, or evaluates scientific measurement error rates.

Formula: (|Experimental - Exact| ÷ Exact) × 100

Example: Error between observed 10.2 and exact 10.0 is 2%.
