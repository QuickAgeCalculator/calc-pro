How to Convert CGPA to Percentage?

Converting Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) into an indicative percentage is a common requirement for higher studies, competitive exams, and job applications. Academic boards like CBSE and universities worldwide utilize specialized multiplier formulas to evaluate student performance accurately.

10

CGPA to Percentage Formula (10 Point Scale)

Under the CBSE and standard Indian academic system, multiply your 10-point CGPA score by 9.5.

Percentage (%) = CGPA × 9.5
4.0

CGPA to Percentage Formula (4 Point Scale)

Common in US universities and WES evaluations. Divide CGPA by 4.0 and multiply by 100.

Percentage (%) = (CGPA / 4.0) × 100
5.0

CGPA to Percentage Formula (5 Point Scale)

Used in European and selected Asian institutions. Divide CGPA by 5.0 and multiply by 100.

Percentage (%) = (CGPA / 5.0) × 100

University-wise CGPA to Percentage Conversion Methods

Different universities establish specific formulas in their academic regulations:

VTU Karnataka Formula: (CGPA - 0.75) × 10
Anna University Formula: CGPA × 10
AKTU / UPTU Formula: (CGPA - 0.75) × 10
GTU Gujarat Formula: (CGPA - 0.50) × 10
Mumbai University Formula: 7.1 or 7.25 × CGPA + 11 (or 9.5)
Delhi University (DU) Formula: CGPA × 9.5

How to Convert Percentage to CGPA?

To reverse the calculation and convert your overall percentage into CGPA on a standard 10-point CBSE scale, simply divide your percentage by 9.5.

Reverse Conversion Formula CGPA = Percentage (%) / 9.5
Example: For 85.5% marks:
85.5 / 9.5 = 9.0 CGPA

Difference Between CGPA and Percentage

While both metrics measure academic standing, they serve distinct evaluation purposes across academic institutions and employment sectors.

Feature / Aspect CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) Percentage (%)
Definition Average of grade points obtained across all subjects/semesters. Total marks obtained divided by maximum total marks × 100.
Scale Range Usually graded on 10.0, 4.0, or 5.0 scales. Fixed scale ranging from 0% to 100%.
Granularity Reduces hyper-competition by grouping performance into grade bands. Provides precise numeric scores down to decimal points.
Global Adoption Preferred by international universities and modern credit systems (CBCS). Traditionally used in competitive entrance exams and scholarship criteria.
