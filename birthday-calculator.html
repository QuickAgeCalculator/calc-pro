How Age & Leap Years Are Calculated

Calculating age seems straightforward, but precise calendar arithmetic requires handling fluctuating month lengths (28, 29, 30, or 31 days) and leap year anomalies.

1. Exact Year & Month Difference: We first compute the difference between the target year/month and birth year/month. If the target day is smaller than the birth day, we borrow a month from the preceding target month, adjusting by the exact number of days in that specific month.

2. Leap Year Rule Handling: A leap year occurs every 4 years, except for end-of-century years unless divisible by 400 (e.g., year 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 was not). For individuals born on February 29, our algorithm dynamically adjusts non-leap year birthdays to February 28 or March 1, ensuring day-accurate progression without date skips.
