Discount Calculation Formulas & Scale Factor

Understanding how percentage discounts work mathematically empowers you to make smarter buying and pricing decisions. Below are the key mathematical formulas utilized by our percentage discount calculator.

What is a Scale Factor?

A scale factor in discount calculations represents the decimal multiplier used to find the final price directly. For instance, a 20% discount leaves 80% of the original cost. The scale factor is calculated as 1 - (Discount % / 100) or 1 - 0.20 = 0.80.

Formula 1

Sale Price Formula

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 – Discount % / 100)

Directly computes what you pay after subtracting the percentage reduction.

Formula 2

Discount Amount Formula

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount % / 100)

Calculates the total monetary savings or cash amount deducted from list price.

Formula 3

Original Price Formula

Original Price = Sale Price ÷ (1 – Discount % / 100)

Reverse-engineers the tag price prior to markdown using the final price.

Types of Promotional Discounts

Percentage Discount

A relative price deduction calculated as a fixed percentage off the retail tag price (e.g., 20% off apparel during seasonal clearance).

Flat Discount

A fixed monetary cash reduction subtracted regardless of total purchase percentage (e.g., $50 flat off orders above $200).

Buy One Get One (BOGO)

Promotions where purchasing an item grants an additional item free or at half price (e.g., Buy 1 Get 1 at 50% = overall 25% discount).

Successive / Stacked Discounts

Multiple consecutive discounts where the second percentage applies after deducting the first (e.g., 20% sale plus extra 10% coupon code).

Seasonal & Festival Offers

Time-sensitive shopping events such as Black Friday, Diwali sale events, or festive holiday promotions featuring bundled percentage price cuts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

To calculate a percentage discount, multiply the original tag price by the discount percentage rate and divide by 100 to get the exact discount amount saved. Afterwards, subtract this discount amount from the original price to reveal your final sale price. Alternatively, multiply the list price directly by (1 minus the discount percentage expressed as a decimal).
To reverse calculate the original price prior to a discount, divide the final sale price by (1 minus the discount rate in decimal form). For instance, if an item costs $80 on sale with a 20% discount offer, divide $80 by 0.80 (which is 1 - 0.20), yielding the original list price of $100.
The discount amount represents the total dollar or rupee savings deducted from the list price. Conversely, the sale price (or net price) is the final amount paid at checkout after deducting the discount amount. For a $100 product with $20 off, $20 is the discount amount and $80 is the sale price.
Successive or stacked discounts apply sequentially rather than adding rates together. First, apply the initial discount percentage to the full original price. Next, calculate the second discount percentage on the newly reduced subtotal. For example, a 20% plus 10% stacked discount equals an effective total discount of 28%, not 30%.

The calculator automatically calculates the discounted price subtotal first and then adds tax to provide your exact total payment figure.
