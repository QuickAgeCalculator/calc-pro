What is Unit Conversion?

Unit conversion is the multi-step mathematical process of changing a quantity expressed in one unit of measurement to an equivalent value in another unit without altering the underlying physical magnitude. Whether converting metric values like kilometers or grams into imperial standards like miles or pounds, unit conversion relies on precise mathematical conversion factors or standardized equations. It enables accurate communication across international trade, engineering, science, travel, and daily cooking recipes where different global systems of measurement coexist.

Unit Conversion Formulas & Factors

Length Conversion Factors

  • 1 inch = 2.54 cm (exact)
  • 1 foot = 0.3048 m
  • 1 mile = 1.609344 km

Weight Conversion Factors

  • 1 pound (lb) = 0.45359237 kg (exact)
  • 1 kilogram (kg) ≈ 2.20462 lbs

Temperature Conversion Formulas

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
K = °C + 273.15

Volume Conversion Factors

  • 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 liters
  • 1 liter ≈ 0.264172 US gallons

Worked Conversion Examples

Example 1: Convert 5 feet to meters

Step: Multiply 5 feet by the conversion factor (0.3048 m / ft).
5 × 0.3048 = 1.524 meters

Example 2: Convert 70 kg to pounds

Step: Multiply 70 kg by 2.20462 lbs / kg.
70 × 2.20462 = 154.3234 pounds

Example 3: Convert 25°C to Fahrenheit

Step: Apply formula °F = (25 × 9/5) + 32.
25 × 1.8 = 45; 45 + 32 = 77°F

Example 4: Convert 2 liters to US gallons

Step: Multiply 2 L by 0.264172 gal / L.
2 × 0.264172 = 0.528344 US gallons

Example 5: Convert 100 km/h to mph

Step: Multiply 100 km/h by 0.621371 mph / (km/h).
100 × 0.621371 = 62.1371 mph

Tips for Accurate Unit Conversion

  • Always check US vs UK liquid measurements: Note that 1 Imperial (UK) gallon equals approximately 4.546 liters, while 1 US liquid gallon equals 3.785 liters.
  • Temperature isn't a direct ratio: Temperature conversions involve offset values (+32 or -273.15), meaning 0°C double does not equal double 0°F.
  • Use high-precision factors: Avoid over-rounding conversion factors early in complex multi-step physics or engineering equations.
  • Observe significant figures: Round final answers to reflect the degree of precision appropriate for your project or homework assignment.
