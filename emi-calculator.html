What is EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)?

Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a financial institution or lender on a fixed date each month. Every EMI payment pays off both the accrued interest and part of the original principal amount over a set number of years or months.

Principal Component

The portion of the EMI directly paying down the original loan balance borrowed.

Interest Component

The fee charged by the lender for borrowing funds, calculated on the outstanding balance.

EMI Calculation Formula

Financial institutions use a mathematical formula based on reducing balance interest rates to determine your exact monthly repayment:

E = [P x r x (1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]
P (Principal): Total loan amount requested from bank.
r (Monthly Rate): Annual Interest Rate / (12 x 100).
n (Tenure Months): Loan duration expressed in total months.

Factors That Affect Your Loan EMI

1. Principal Loan Amount

The total capital borrowed directly impacts monthly EMI. Higher amounts result in higher monthly commitments.

2. Interest Rate

The rate charged by lenders. Lower interest rates lead to reduced interest burdens and cheaper EMIs.

3. Loan Tenure

A longer tenure lowers your monthly payment but significantly increases total cumulative interest paid over time.

4. Credit Score & Type

A high credit score (750+) unlocks preferential interest rates. Choice of fixed vs floating rates also alters repayments.

Smart Tips to Reduce Your EMI

Increase Down Payment

Pay more upfront to reduce principal borrowing and lower monthly EMI.

Optimise Loan Tenure

Choose a tenure that balances manageable EMI with minimal total interest payout.

Compare Lenders

Always negotiate rates across multiple banks to lock in lowest APR.

Partial Prepayments

Make occasional lump sum prepayments to cut down remaining tenure and interest.
