What is EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)?

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified date each calendar month. EMIs are applied to both interest and principal each month so that over a specified number of years, the loan is fully paid off along with interest.

Principal Component

This is the actual portion of the monthly payment that goes directly towards reducing the original loan amount borrowed. In the early years, the principal component is smaller and grows larger over time.

Interest Component

This component covers the cost of borrowing charged by the lender. In the initial loan period, interest forms the largest share of your EMI installment.

Loan EMI Calculation Formula

Most financial institutions and banks use the standard mathematical reducing-balance formula to calculate loan EMIs:

E = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ - 1)
E Monthly EMI Amount
P Principal Loan Amount
r Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
n Loan Tenure in Months

Factors That Affect Your Loan EMI

1. Principal Loan Amount

Higher principal amount directly increases the monthly EMI installment and overall interest burden over the loan lifecycle.

2. Interest Rate

The interest rate charged by lenders heavily determines the cost of borrowing. Even a 0.5% rate reduction leads to significant savings.

3. Loan Tenure

Longer tenure reduces your monthly EMI cash outflow but increases total compounding interest paid to the lender.

4. Credit Score & Type

A high CIBIL score (750+) qualifies you for lower interest rates, reducing overall EMI burdens compared to standard rates.

Smart Tips to Reduce Your EMI

Increase Down Payment

Pay a higher initial down payment to lower the required principal loan amount.

Optimise Loan Tenure

Partial Prepayments

Make annual principal prepayments when receiving bonuses to rapidly shrink tenure and total interest.
