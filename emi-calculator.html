What is EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)?

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is the fixed financial amount paid by a borrower to a lender every month on a specified date. EMIs are applied to both interest and principal until the loan is paid off in full over the specified loan tenure.

Principal Component

The Principal amount represents the actual capital borrowed from the bank. In the initial years of a loan, a smaller portion of your monthly EMI goes toward reducing the principal balance. As tenure progresses, the principal share increases significantly.

>

Interest Component

The Interest rate component is the fee charged by the lender for borrowing funds. Calculated using the Reducing balance method, interest is highest during initial payments because the outstanding principal balance is highest.

Loan EMI Calculation Formula

Banks and financial calculators use the standard mathematical reducing balance formula to compute Equated Monthly Installments:

E = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ - 1)
E Monthly EMI Amount
P Principal Loan Amount
r Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
n Loan Tenure in Months

Factors That Affect Your Loan EMI

Your monthly repayment amount is governed by three primary variables: the principal loan amount, the effective interest rate, and the loan tenure. Additionally, maintaining a strong CIBIL score (750+) ensures you secure lower interest rates from leading banks like SBI, HDFC, and ICICI Bank.

1. Principal Loan Amount

The total capital borrowed. A higher loan principal directly increases monthly EMI payments and total interest payable.

2. Interest Rate & Type

Whether fixed or floating interest rate, even a 0.25% reduction in interest rates can save lakhs on long-term home loans.

3. Loan Tenure

Longer tenures lower your monthly outflow but increase overall accumulated interest costs significantly.

4. CIBIL Score Impact on EMI

A CIBIL score above 750 qualifies you for prime interest rate discounts from leading banks like SBI, HDFC, and ICICI.
