What is a Work Day / Business Day?

A working day (or business day) is officially defined as any official day of the week on which regular commercial operations, office work, and banking transactions take place. In most western and modern corporate frameworks, a standard 5-day work week runs from Monday through Friday.

Non-Working Days Include:

  • Official Weekends: Standard Saturdays and Sundays (or Friday/Saturday depending on region).
  • Public / Federal / Bank Holidays: Nationally or locally recognized non-working official days.
  • Company Off-Days: Custom corporate shutdowns, leave periods, or PTO days.

How the Calculator Works / Formula

Our working day calculator uses an exact mathematical date-iteration algorithm that mirrors advanced spreadsheet functions like NETWORKDAYS.INTL.

// Conceptual Business Day Formula

Net Working Days = Total Calendar Days - (Weekend Days + Public Holidays + Custom Off-Days)

// Total Working Hours Formula

Total Working Hours = Net Working Days × Daily Work Hours (e.g., 8.0 hrs)

Weekdays vs. Working Days

A weekday refers strictly to any day that is not Saturday or Sunday (Monday through Friday). A working day excludes weekdays that happen to fall on recognized public or national holidays. Thus, all working days are weekdays, but not all weekdays are working days.

Work Days vs. Calendar Days

Calendar days measure standard consecutive 24-hour periods (365 days a year). Work days filter out non-operational calendar days so businesses can calculate realistic turnarounds for shipping, legal contracts, and project delivery.

Are the Start Date and End Date Included?

By default in standard business day calculations, the start date is considered day zero, while the end date is included in the count. However, our business days calculator allows full customizable toggling so you can include or exclude start and end dates based on your specific organizational guidelines.
