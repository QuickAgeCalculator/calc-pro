What is BMI (Body Mass Index)?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple numerical calculation that uses an individual's height and weight to estimate overall body fatness. First devised by Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s, BMI has evolved into an internationally recognized screening metric adopted by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

While BMI does not measure adipose tissue directly, research shows it correlates strongly with more invasive direct fat measurements (such as underwater weighing or dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry). Healthcare professionals widely use BMI as an economical, non-invasive first line of evaluation to identify potential weight issues in adults.

BMI Formula

BMI is calculated by dividing your mass by the square of your height. Depending on your choice of measurement system, use one of the two standard mathematical formulas below:

Metric System
BMI = weight (kg) / [height (m)]²

Example: A person weighing 70 kg and standing 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 / (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86 kg/m².

Imperial System
BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) / [height (in)]²

Example: A person weighing 154 lbs and standing 69 inches tall has a BMI of 703 × 154 / (69 × 69) = 22.73.

BMI Categories Chart (WHO Standard)

The World Health Organization categorizes adult weight status according to fixed BMI thresholds. These categories help identify potential risks for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and other metabolic conditions.

Below 18.5 Underweight Increased risk
18.5 – 24.9 Normal weight Low / Optimal
25.0 – 29.9 Overweight Increased risk
30.0 – 34.9 Obese Class I High risk
35.0 – 39.9 Obese Class II Very High risk
40.0 and above Obese Class III Extremely High risk

Limitations of BMI

While BMI serves as a useful statistical tool for population studies, it has recognized limitations when evaluating individual health:

  • Does not differentiate muscle from fat: Muscle tissue is denser than fat. Bodybuilders and trained athletes can record high BMIs despite having very low body fat percentages.
  • Does not consider fat distribution: Visceral fat around abdominal organs carries higher cardiometabolic risk than subcutaneous fat stored elsewhere.
  • Ethnic Differences: Research indicates that health risks associated with obesity may manifest at lower BMI thresholds (e.g., 23.0 for overweight) in individuals of Asian descent.
  • Age & Pregnancy: Muscle loss in older adults can mask excessive fatness, while pregnant and lactating women naturally experience weight changes not reflected accurately by BMI.

BMI for Men, Women & Different Age Groups

BMI Calculator for Women

Women naturally carry a higher percentage of essential body fat than men due to reproductive biology. Although standard WHO adult BMI ranges remain the same across genders, women generally store more subcutaneous fat at identical BMI values.

BMI Calculator for Men

Men typically possess higher bone density and muscle mass than women. As a result, men may occasionally register slightly higher BMIs without carrying excessive adipose tissue.

BMI for Adults vs Children / Teens

This calculator is intended for adults aged 20 and older. For children and adolescents under 20, raw numerical BMI is not used directly. Instead, health professionals convert BMI into an age- and sex-specific percentile (CDC growth charts) to account for natural childhood growth spurts.
