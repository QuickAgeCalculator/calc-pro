What is BMI (Body Mass Index)?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a standardized medical numerical measurement used to evaluate whether an individual has a healthy weight status relative to their height. Developed by Belgian polymath Adolphe Quetelet in the 19th century, BMI provides a straightforward screening technique used globally by doctors, health organizations, and fitness specialists.

BMI helps classify individuals into specific body weight categories, including Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight, and Obese. By categorizing body mass, individuals and medical professionals can identify potential health risks associated with high body fat percentage or excessive undernourishment.

BMI Formula

The standard mathematical formula calculates your body mass index by dividing total weight in kilograms by height in meters squared ($\text{kg/m}^2$).

Metric BMI Formula

$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight (kg)}}{\left[\text{Height (m)}\right]^2}$$

Example: For a person weighing $70\text{ kg}$ with a height of $1.75\text{ m}$ ($175\text{ cm}$): $70 / (1.75 \times 1.75) = 22.86\text{ kg/m}^2$.

Imperial BMI Formula

$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight (lbs)} \times 703}{\left[\text{Height (inches)}\right]^2}$$

Example: For a person weighing $154\text{ lbs}$ standing $69\text{ inches}$ tall: $(154 \times 703) / (69 \times 69) = 22.73$.

BMI Categories Chart (WHO Standard)

Below is the official World Health Organization (WHO) adult weight status classification table used by medical practitioners worldwide:

< 18.5 Underweight Increased risk for nutritional deficiency & immunity issues
18.5 – 24.9 Normal Weight Lowest risk for chronic metabolic complications
25.0 – 29.9 Overweight Moderate risk for cardiovascular strain and hypertension
30.0 – 34.9 Obese (Class I) High risk for type 2 diabetes and heart conditions
35.0 – 39.9 Obese (Class II) Very high risk for cardiovascular complications
≥ 40.0 Obese (Class III / Severe) Extremely critical medical health risk

Limitations of BMI

While the standard Body Mass Index Calculator provides an excellent general population metric, it has essential physiological limitations:

  • Muscle Mass vs Fat Mass BMI does not differentiate between lean muscle mass and adipose body fat. Trained athletes or bodybuilders may score as "overweight" despite extremely low fat levels.
  • Age & Bone Density Factor Older adults naturally lose bone density and lean mass while gaining visceral body fat, which may understate health risks at normal BMI ratings.
  • Fat Distribution & Waist Size BMI ignores fat distribution. Abdominal visceral fat around organs carries higher risk than subcutaneous fat on limbs, regardless of BMI score.
  • Ethnic Variations Different ethnic populations (e.g., South Asian vs. Caucasian backgrounds) experience metabolic risk factors at varying BMI thresholds.

BMI for Men, Women & Different Age Groups

BMI Calculator for Women

Women naturally carry a higher percentage of essential body fat than men due to reproductive biology. Although standard WHO adult BMI ranges remain the same across genders, women generally store more subcutaneous fat at identical BMI values.

BMI Calculator for Men

Men typically possess higher bone density and muscle mass than women. As a result, men may occasionally register slightly higher BMIs without carrying excessive adipose tissue.

Child and Pediatric BMI Percentiles

For children and teens aged 2 through 19, BMI is calculated using age and sex-specific growth percentiles (CDC/WHO growth charts) because body fat composition alters rapidly during physical growth stages.
