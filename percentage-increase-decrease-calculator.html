Percentage Increase & Decrease Formula

To understand how to calculate percentage increase and decrease manually, you need to understand the relationship between the original value, new value, absolute change, and relative change.

Percentage Change Formula:

Percentage Change = ((New Value – Original Value) / Original Value) × 100

Short form LSI notation: ((new – old) / old) × 100

Percent Increase Formula

When the new value is larger than the original value, you experience positive percentage growth:

Percent Increase = ((New – Old) / Old) × 100

Percent Decrease Formula

When the final value is lower than the initial number, you experience a percentage decline:

Percent Decrease = ((Old – New) / Old) × 100

Discount Calculator Formula

Calculating a discount involves determining the decrease by percentage on an item's original sticker price using a simple multiplier:

Amount Saved ($) = Original Price × (Discount Rate / 100)
Final Discounted Price ($) = Original Price – Amount Saved
Price Multiplier = (100 - Discount Rate) / 100

Real-Life Calculation Examples

Example 1: Salary Percentage Increase Calculator

Suppose your salary increases from old value to new value of $50,000 to $58,000 per year. What is your rate of change?

  • Absolute Change = $58,000 - $50,000 = $8,000
  • Relative Change = 8,000 / 50,000 = 0.16
  • Percent Growth = 0.16 × 100 = 16% Salary Increase

Example 2: Price Percentage Increase Decrease Calculator

A store product drops in price from $80 down to $60 during a promotional sale.

  • Absolute Change = $60 - $80 = -$20
  • Relative Change = -20 / 80 = -0.25
  • Percentage Decline = 25% Price Decrease

Difference Between Percentage Increase, Percentage Change & Discount

Concept Primary Purpose Formula / Direction Typical Application
Percentage Change Measures two-way relative change between old & new value ((New - Old) / Old) × 100 Stock market, population, metric shifts
Percentage Increase Measures positive growth rate from initial value + Growth (New > Old) Salary raise, revenue growth, inflation
Discount Measures specific price reduction applied to retail cost Original × (1 - Rate) E-commerce, retail shopping sales, coupons

Common Mistakes While Calculating Percentage Increase/Decrease & Discounts

  • Dividing by the New Value instead of Original Value: Always divide the difference by the starting original value, not the final number.
  • Confusing Percent Points with Percent Change: Going from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative percentage increase!
  • Assuming Reversibility: A 50% decrease followed by a 50% increase does NOT return you to the original value (e.g., $100 - 50% = $50; $50 + 50% = $75).
