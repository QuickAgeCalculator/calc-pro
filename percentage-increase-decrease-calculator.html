Percentage Increase & Decrease Formula

To measure the proportional shift between an initial value ($V_{old}$) and a final value ($V_{new}$), mathematical formulas evaluate the relative difference against the starting base:

Percentage Change = ((New Value − Old Value) / Old Value) × 100

New Value After Increase = Original Value × (1 + Percentage / 100)

New Value After Decrease = Original Value × (1 − Percentage / 100)

Percentage Increase Example

Suppose an employee's hourly wage increases from $20 to $25. What is the total percentage increase?

Step 1: Difference = 25 − 20 = 5
Step 2: Divide by Old Value = 5 / 20 = 0.25
Step 3: Multiply by 100 = 0.25 × 100 = +25% Percentage Increase

Percentage Decrease Example

A laptop's price drops from $800 down to $600 during a promotional clearance sale. What is the total percentage decrease?

Step 1: Difference = 600 − 800 = -200
Step 2: Divide by Old Value = -200 / 800 = -0.25
Step 3: Multiply by 100 = -0.25 × 100 = -25% Percentage Decrease

Reverse Calculation (Find Original Value)

If a product costs $120 after experiencing a 20% price hike, what was the initial original price?

Formula: Original = Final / (1 + Rate/100)
Original = 120 / (1 + 0.20) = 120 / 1.20 = $100 Original Price

Discount Calculator Formula

Calculating promotional discounts requires determining the exact savings amount subtracted from retail sticker price:

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount % / 100)

Sale Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

Reverse Original Price = Sale Price / (1 − Discount % / 100)

Discount Example (With Numbers)

A jacket listed at $150 is offered at a 30% percent off discount.

Discount Amount = $150 × (30 / 100) = $45 Savings
Final Sale Price = $150 − $45 = $105 Checkout Price

Multiple Discounts / Stacked Coupons

When combining a 20% store sale with an extra 10% coupon code on a $100 item, percentages are non-additive:

After 1st Discount (20%): $100 − $20 = $80
After 2nd Discount (10% of $80): $80 − $8 = $72 Final Price
Effective Combined Discount = 28% Off (Not 30%)

Difference Between Percentage Increase, Percentage Change & Discount

While these statistical terms overlap, understanding their distinct applications prevents confusion in financial reporting:

Metric Concept Primary Focus Direction Typical Context
Percentage Increase Growth relative to original base Positive (+) Upward Salary raises, inflation, profits
Percentage Change Overall relative shift magnitude Bi-directional (+ or −) Stock price shifts, weight change
Shopping Discount Retail price reduction markdown Negative (−) Downward Coupons, retail sales, savings

Common Mistakes While Calculating Percentage Increase/Decrease & Discounts

  • Using the Wrong Base Value: Always divide difference by the original starting value, not the final new value. Dividing by the wrong number severely distorts percentage calculations.
  • Confusing Percentage Points with Percentage: Moving interest rates from 4% to 5% is a 1 percentage point increase, but a 25% relative percentage increase.
  • Adding Multiple Discount Coupons Linearly: Stacking a 20% coupon and a 10% coupon equals 28% total savings, not 30%, because the second discount applies to the already discounted subtotal.
  • Assuming Reversibility is Equal: If a $100 stock drops by 50% to $50, it requires a 100% percentage increase (not 50%) just to return to its original $100 baseline.
